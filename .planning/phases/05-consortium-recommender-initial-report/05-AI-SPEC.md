## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Rules-Based Decision System (deterministic combinatorial scorer with constraint-satisfaction exclusion logic)

**Description:**
A deterministic, rules-based consortium recommender for UK civil nuclear and defence bid planning. Given a requested capability mix (e.g. one Consultancy + one Infrastructure + one Nuclear firm), the system generates all valid firm combinations from a seeded pool of 12 firms, hard-excludes any combination that contains a flagged conflict-of-interest pair, scores remaining combinations across four weighted signals (contract relevance, UK ownership/NSI proxy, past teaming evidence, risk flags), and returns the top 3 ranked teams with a per-signal score breakdown per firm. Foreign ownership and NSI (National Security and Investment Act 2021) considerations are surfaced as badges on each recommended firm. The user can lock specific firms and recompute. No LLM is in the scoring path — all outputs are deterministic and fully auditable.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
<!-- Researched by gsd-domain-researcher — see Section 1b for domain context -->
1. **Silent conflict omission**: A conflicted firm pair (e.g. auditor–auditee or direct competitors) passes through to a recommended team because the conflict exclusion set was built from stale or incomplete data. The bid manager submits a consortium that is disqualified at ITT stage or creates a procurement integrity issue. This is the highest-stakes failure — the system's core promise is that conflicted pairs are never co-recommended.
2. **NSI flag suppressed or missing**: A foreign-owned firm (e.g. non-UK ultimate parent) is recommended without surfacing its NSI/ownership flag. The bid team proceeds without awareness of the security-vetting implication, potentially breaching MoD security requirements or triggering an NSI Act notification obligation they were not prepared for.
3. **Explainability gap — score without evidence**: The system returns a ranked team but the per-signal score breakdown is absent, miscalculated, or not traceable to the underlying DB records. A bid manager cannot verify why Firm X scored above Firm Y and cannot defend the recommendation in an internal governance review. This violates CONS-02 and the "no unsourced assertions" invariant established across Phases 1–4.
4. **No-valid-team returned silently**: When no combination satisfies capability coverage + conflict exclusion constraints, the system returns an empty recommendations array with no status or reason. The user sees nothing and cannot determine whether the gap is a data problem, a capability gap in the firm pool, or a constraint conflict — leading to wasted manual investigation time.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** UK Government Procurement — Civil Nuclear and Defence

**User Population:** Bid managers and business development leads at firms competing for UK government contracts in civil nuclear (e.g. Sizewell C, SMR programmes) and defence (MoD capital programmes). Single-user internal tooling; the decision-maker is a senior practitioner who will act on the recommendation directly when composing a consortium bid response.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** The recommended consortium is used to decide which firms to approach for teaming agreements ahead of submitting a bid. A wrong recommendation can cause: (1) bid disqualification at ITT/selection stage due to an undisclosed conflict of interest between consortium members; (2) a security vetting failure or NSI Act notification obligation triggered by a foreign-owned partner the team was not prepared for; (3) internal governance challenge when the recommendation cannot be audited back to evidence. Downstream, a failed consortium selection can result in the firm not bidding, bidding with a weakened team, or winning and then losing the contract on delivery due to capability gaps.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension:** Conflict-of-interest completeness
Good (bid manager accepts): Every firm pair in the recommended team has been checked against the conflict registry; any known audit relationship or direct-competitor relationship is excluded, and the reasoning states which pairs were excluded and why.
Bad (bid manager flags): A firm that audits another team member, or a firm explicitly flagged as a competitor in the research findings, appears in the same recommended consortium without any conflict badge or exclusion note.
Stakes: Critical
Source: UK Procurement Act 2023 s.26 (exclusion grounds — conflicts of interest); MoD procurement integrity requirements; established bid management practice — conflict disclosure is mandatory in ITT responses.

---

**Dimension:** NSI / foreign-ownership transparency
Good (bid manager accepts): Each recommended firm shows its UK incorporation status, ultimate parent country, and a clear NSI flag where the parent is non-UK — with the Companies House PSC source cited. The bid manager can immediately identify which firms require additional due diligence or notification under the NSI Act 2021.
Bad (bid manager flags): A firm with a non-UK ultimate parent (e.g. French, US, or Chinese-owned) is recommended without any ownership badge, or the ownership is shown as "unknown" when Companies House PSC data was available and not queried.
Stakes: Critical
Source: National Security and Investment Act 2021 — mandatory notification in 17 sensitive sectors including civil nuclear and defence; Procurement Act 2023 national security exclusion grounds; MoD DSP (Defence Sourcing Portal) supplier vetting requirements.

---

**Dimension:** Score explainability — per-signal auditability
Good (bid manager accepts): For each firm in the recommended team, the system shows the score on each of the four signals (contract relevance, UK ownership, past teaming, risk flags) separately, with the weight applied and the underlying evidence (e.g. "3 UK procurement awards in last 5 years", "1 high-severity risk flag"). A peer reviewer or governance panel can trace the recommendation back to specific DB records.
Bad (bid manager flags): The system returns a ranked team with a single composite score per firm but no breakdown by signal. The bid manager cannot tell whether a firm scored highly because of strong contract history or because there are no risk flags — and cannot defend the ranking in a bid review meeting.
Stakes: High
Source: CONS-02 requirement; established bid governance practice — consortium decisions in regulated procurement must be defensible and documented; Cabinet Office spend controls guidance on AI-assisted decision support.

---

**Dimension:** Capability coverage — no silent gaps
Good (bid manager accepts): The recommended team collectively covers every capability tag in the requested mix. Each firm's contribution to the team's capability coverage is explicitly shown (e.g. "Alpha Nuclear Ltd — Nuclear tag"). If a tag cannot be covered by any non-conflicted firm in the pool, the system states this explicitly.
Bad (bid manager flags): The recommended team is missing a requested capability tag (e.g. no Nuclear-tagged firm when one was requested) because the capability coverage check silently passed or used a partial match. The bid manager submits a consortium that fails the minimum capability threshold at PQQ stage.
Stakes: High
Source: UK public procurement practice — PQQ/SQ (Selection Questionnaire) minimum requirements are capability-gated; a consortium that cannot demonstrate collective capability coverage fails selection before evaluation begins.

---

**Dimension:** Evidence provenance — sourced assertions only
Good (bid manager accepts): Any claim surfaced in the recommendation output (ownership status, risk flag, past teaming evidence, contract history) carries a source: a URL, a Companies House record reference, or a cited AI research finding with the raw snippet. The bid manager can verify any assertion before acting on it.
Bad (bid manager flags): The recommendation states "Firm X has prior teaming experience with Firm Y" or "Firm Z has no NSI concerns" without a source. The bid manager acts on an unsourced assertion that later proves incorrect — reputational and procurement-integrity risk.
Stakes: High
Source: Project-wide invariant established in Phase 1 (RES-03, RES-04) and carried through Phase 4; established practice in UK procurement compliance — unsourced bid intelligence is not auditable.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Stale conflict data producing false recommendations**: In UK nuclear and defence markets, firm relationships change frequently (JVs dissolve, audit contracts rotate, firms merge). If the conflict registry is not refreshed when firms are re-researched, a conflict that emerged after the last refresh will not be caught. The system has no mechanism to warn the user that conflict data is stale — a last-researched timestamp per firm is the only signal.

2. **Name-matching false positives/negatives in conflict and partnership detection**: UK nuclear and defence has many similarly-named subsidiaries and JV vehicles (e.g. "Rolls-Royce SMR Ltd" vs "Rolls-Royce plc"). If the Phase 4 extraction matched conflict or partnership records by name rather than Companies House number, the recommender may fail to detect a real conflict (false negative) or flag a non-existent one (false positive). Bid managers in this sector are sensitive to both errors — a missed conflict is a compliance risk; a false conflict excludes a strong partner unnecessarily.

3. **NSI Act scope under-awareness**: The NSI Act 2021 applies to 17 sensitive sectors including civil nuclear and advanced materials. A firm may be UK-incorporated but ultimately owned by a non-UK entity via a holding chain not visible in the Companies House PSC data (e.g. a UK subsidiary of a US private equity fund). The system's ownership scorer only has the depth of the PSC data retrieved in Phase 3 — it cannot guarantee full ownership chain visibility, and a bid manager may interpret a "UK incorporated" flag as full NSI clearance when it is not.

4. **Weight-slider gaming producing compliance-unsafe outputs**: The user can set the UK ownership weight to zero, which allows a heavily foreign-owned firm to rank top regardless of NSI risk. In a civil nuclear or defence context, a bid manager may not recognise this as a problem. The system surfaces NSI flags per firm regardless of weights, but there is no guardrail preventing a weight configuration that systematically down-ranks UK-owned firms.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**UK Procurement Act 2023** (in force February 2025): Replaces DSPCR 2011 for competed defence contracts. Introduces mandatory exclusion grounds including conflicts of interest (s.26) and national security (s.14). Consortium bids must disclose conflicts between consortium members. Relevant to: conflict exclusion logic (CONS-03), NSI surfacing (CONS-04).

**National Security and Investment Act 2021** (NSI Act, in force January 2022): Mandatory notification for acquisitions in 17 sensitive sectors including civil nuclear, defence, and advanced materials. Country-agnostic — applies to UK and foreign actors equally. No minimum threshold. Relevant to: foreign ownership flags on recommended firms (CONS-04); the system surfaces NSI considerations but does not constitute legal advice on notification obligations.

**MoD Defence Sourcing Portal (DSP) supplier vetting**: Suppliers and consortium members on MoD programmes must hold or be capable of obtaining appropriate security clearances (SC, DV as applicable). Foreign-owned firms face additional scrutiny. Relevant to: ownership badge display and NSI flag on each recommended firm.

**Civil Nuclear Security Regulations**: ONR (Office for Nuclear Regulation) security vetting requirements apply to firms working on civil nuclear sites. Foreign ownership is a material factor in vetting outcomes. Relevant to: NSI and ownership signal weighting.

**No GDPR or data-subject obligations apply** to this system: all data is about firms (legal entities), not individuals, and sourced from public domain APIs (Companies House, Contracts Finder, Find a Tender).

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Plain TypeScript scoring module — no AI/agent framework. Zod 4 for request/response validation. Vitest for unit testing the scoring algorithm.

**Version:** TypeScript 5.6+ (already in project), Zod 4 (already in project), Vitest 4 (already in devDependencies)

**Rationale:**
Phase 5 is a deterministic rules-based consortium scorer: a weighted sum of four DB-sourced signals (contract history, capability tags, ownership flags, conflict registry) plus hard exclusion of conflicted firm pairs. The requirements mandate full explainability and per-signal auditable score breakdown (CONS-02). An LLM in the scoring path would make results non-reproducible and unverifiable — directly undermining the core requirement. No AI framework from the matrix applies: there is no retrieval corpus, no multi-agent orchestration, no autonomous planning, and no NL generation in the recommendation path. The Vercel AI SDK / Claude stack remains scoped to the upstream Phase 1-4 research pipeline.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| Vercel AI SDK 5 (`generateObject` + `@ai-sdk/anthropic`) | Reserve only for an optional future Claude-written prose narrative layered on top of scores — never inside scoring/ranking. Would make results non-reproducible and fail CONS-02 explainability. |
| LangGraph / LangChain | Over-engineered; no agentic orchestration needed for a weighted-sum scorer. |
| LlamaIndex | RAG-oriented; no retrieval corpus in Phase 5 — all signals come from structured Postgres tables. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — plain TypeScript with no proprietary framework dependencies in Phase 5 recommendation path.

### Entry Point Pattern

```typescript
// src/app/api/recommend/route.ts
// POST /api/recommend — validates input, loads DB signals, runs scorer, returns typed response

import { NextRequest, NextResponse } from "next/server";
import { db } from "@/db/client";
import { firms, conflictPairs, partnershipPairs, ownershipRecords, riskFlags, procurementRecords } from "@/db/schema";
import { RecommendRequestSchema, RecommendResponseSchema } from "@/lib/recommend/schemas";
import { scoreAndRankCombinations } from "@/lib/recommend/scorer";

export async function POST(req: NextRequest) {
  // 1. Parse and validate request body with Zod 4
  const body = await req.json();
  const parsed = RecommendRequestSchema.safeParse(body);
  if (!parsed.success) {
    return NextResponse.json({ error: "Invalid request", details: parsed.error.flatten() }, { status: 400 });
  }
  const { capabilityMix, lockedFirmIds, weights } = parsed.data;

  // 2. Load all signals from DB (read-only — never mutates these tables)
  const [allFirms, allConflicts, allPartnerships, allOwnership, allRiskFlags, allProcurement] =
    await Promise.all([
      db.select().from(firms),
      db.select().from(conflictPairs),
      db.select().from(partnershipPairs),
      db.select().from(ownershipRecords),
      db.select().from(riskFlags),
      db.select().from(procurementRecords),
    ]);

  // 3. Run deterministic scorer (pure function — no side effects, fully testable)
  const result = scoreAndRankCombinations({
    capabilityMix,
    lockedFirmIds,
    weights,
    firms: allFirms,
    conflicts: allConflicts,
    partnerships: allPartnerships,
    ownership: allOwnership,
    riskFlags: allRiskFlags,
    procurement: allProcurement,
  });

  // 4. Validate response shape before sending
  const validated = RecommendResponseSchema.parse(result);
  return NextResponse.json(validated);
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `RecommendRequestSchema` (Zod 4) | Validates the POST body: `capabilityMix`, `lockedFirmIds`, `weights` (four signal sliders). Uses `z.object()` with `.safeParse()` for error-safe validation — never `.parse()` on untrusted input. | At the start of the API route handler, before any DB query. |
| `RecommendResponseSchema` (Zod 4) | Validates the scorer's output before it leaves the server: typed `TeamRecommendation[]` with `firmScoreBreakdown` per firm per signal. Ensures the response contract is never accidentally broken by scorer changes. | After `scoreAndRankCombinations()` returns, before `NextResponse.json()`. |
| `scoreAndRankCombinations()` (pure function) | Takes all DB signals as plain objects, generates all valid firm combinations for the requested `capabilityMix`, applies hard conflict exclusion, computes weighted scores, returns top 3 ranked teams. No DB access, no side effects. | In `route.ts` after loading all signals. The pure-function boundary is what makes unit testing cheap. |
| Conflict exclusion set | A `Set<string>` of canonical pair keys (`"${minId}__${maxId}"`) built once from `conflictPairs`. O(1) lookup per pair during combination generation. Never use array `.find()` inside a combinatorial loop — O(n) per check degrades badly at scale. | Inside `scoreAndRankCombinations()`, built once before the combination loop. |
| Signal scorer per firm | Four deterministic functions — `scoreContractRelevance()`, `scoreCapabilityMatch()`, `scoreCapacity()`, `scorePastTeaming()` — each taking a firm's signals and returning `0.0–1.0`. Weights are applied by the caller, not inside the scorer. Keeps each scorer independently testable. | Called per firm inside the combination evaluator. |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Iterating conflict pairs inside the combination loop.** If you call `conflicts.find(c => ...)` for every firm pair in every combination, you get O(combinations × conflicts) — for 12 firms choosing 3, that is 220 combinations × up to 66 pairs = 14,520 lookups per request. Build the conflict exclusion `Set` once outside the loop using canonical pair keys: `"${[a,b].sort().join('__')}"`.

2. **Generating combinations with `JSON.parse(JSON.stringify())` for deep copies inside the loop.** Cloning objects in a tight combinatorial loop is slow and unnecessary. Work with firm IDs through the combination generation phase; resolve to full firm objects only once a combination passes conflict exclusion and is about to be scored.

3. **Putting the Zod schema in the route file.** When the schema is co-located with the handler it cannot be imported by Vitest tests without importing the Next.js route module, which pulls in the DB client. Keep `schemas.ts` and `scorer.ts` in `src/lib/recommend/` — no Next.js dependencies — so tests run in pure Node with no mocking.

4. **Returning a silent empty array when no valid team exists.** CONTEXT.md D-07 and the codebase's "not found is explicit" invariant both require an explicit `{ status: "no_valid_team", reason: string }` response. Never return an empty `recommendations: []` without a status field — the UI will silently show nothing and the user will not know why.

5. **Mutating the DB tables inside the scorer.** The `conflictPairs`, `partnershipPairs`, `ownershipRecords`, `riskFlags`, and `procurementRecords` tables are owned by Phases 1–4. Phase 5 reads from them exclusively. Any write to these tables from the recommender path would break the Phase 4 extraction invariants (delete-then-insert pattern for `riskFlags`, unique-constraint upserts for others).

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:** None. Phase 5 is a deterministic rules-based scorer with no LLM calls in the recommendation path. All computation uses plain TypeScript arithmetic on DB-sourced signal values.

**Scoring Algorithm Architecture:**

The algorithm is a weighted sum of four normalised signals per firm, with hard conflict exclusion applied before scoring. Each signal returns a value in `[0.0, 1.0]`. Weights are user-supplied (0–100 per signal, normalised to sum to 1.0 before multiplication).

```typescript
// src/lib/recommend/scorer.ts
// Pure function — no DB access, no side effects. All inputs are plain objects.

import type { Firm, ConflictPair, PartnershipPair, OwnershipRecord, RiskFlag, ProcurementRecord } from "@/lib/types";
import type { CapabilityMix, ScoringWeights, TeamRecommendation, FirmScoreBreakdown } from "./schemas";

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// Signal scorers — each returns 0.0–1.0
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

/**
 * Contract relevance: proportion of procurement records with an awardDate in the last
 * 5 years. Rewards recent, active contract delivery over stale history.
 *
 * Returns 0 if the firm has no procurement records (not punished by default — use the
 * weight slider to reduce this signal's contribution when data is sparse).
 */
export function scoreContractRelevance(
  firmId: string,
  procurement: ProcurementRecord[],
  asOf = new Date(),
): number {
  const firmRecords = procurement.filter((r) => r.firmId === firmId);
  if (firmRecords.length === 0) return 0;

  const cutoff = new Date(asOf);
  cutoff.setFullYear(cutoff.getFullYear() - 5);

  const recent = firmRecords.filter(
    (r) => r.awardDate != null && new Date(r.awardDate) >= cutoff,
  ).length;

  return recent / firmRecords.length; // normalised fraction
}

/**
 * Capability match: fraction of the requested capability tags that this firm covers.
 * A firm that covers all requested tags scores 1.0; partial coverage scores proportionally.
 */
export function scoreCapabilityMatch(
  firm: Firm,
  requestedTags: string[],
): number {
  if (requestedTags.length === 0) return 1.0;
  const firmTags: string[] = JSON.parse(firm.capabilityTags ?? "[]");
  const matched = requestedTags.filter((tag) => firmTags.includes(tag)).length;
  return matched / requestedTags.length;
}

/**
 * UK ownership / NSI proxy: 1.0 = confirmed UK-incorporated, 0.5 = unknown, 0.0 = confirmed foreign.
 * Higher weight on this signal reflects NSI preference (CONS-04).
 * Foreign-owned firms are surfaced with a badge but never hard-excluded (D-03 decision).
 */
export function scoreUkOwnership(firmId: string, ownership: OwnershipRecord[]): number {
  const record = ownership.find((o) => o.firmId === firmId);
  if (!record) return 0.5; // unknown = neutral
  if (record.ukIncorporated === "yes") return 1.0;
  if (record.ukIncorporated === "no") return 0.0;
  return 0.5;
}

/**
 * Risk flags: penalise firms with high-severity flags.
 * Score = 1.0 − (weighted flag burden / max burden).
 * Severity weights: High=3, Medium=2, Low=1.
 */
export function scoreRiskFlags(firmId: string, riskFlags: RiskFlag[]): number {
  const firmFlags = riskFlags.filter((f) => f.firmId === firmId);
  if (firmFlags.length === 0) return 1.0;

  const severityWeight: Record<string, number> = { High: 3, Medium: 2, Low: 1 };
  const burden = firmFlags.reduce((sum, f) => sum + (severityWeight[f.severity] ?? 1), 0);
  const maxBurden = firmFlags.length * 3; // worst case: all High

  return 1.0 - burden / maxBurden;
}

/**
 * Past teaming: 1.0 if any partnership pair exists between two firms in the combination,
 * 0.0 if none. Applied at the team level (not per-firm) — any prior co-delivery
 * between two members lifts the team's partnership score.
 */
export function teamHasPriorPartnerships(
  teamFirmIds: string[],
  partnerships: PartnershipPair[],
): boolean {
  for (let i = 0; i < teamFirmIds.length; i++) {
    for (let j = i + 1; j < teamFirmIds.length; j++) {
      const [a, b] = [teamFirmIds[i], teamFirmIds[j]].sort();
      const found = partnerships.some(
        (p) => [p.firmAId, p.firmBId].sort().join() === [a, b].join(),
      );
      if (found) return true;
    }
  }
  return false;
}

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// Conflict exclusion — O(1) lookup via Set
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

/**
 * Build a Set of canonical conflict pair keys for O(1) exclusion during combination
 * generation. Key format: "${minId}__${maxId}" (lexicographic ordering).
 */
export function buildConflictSet(conflicts: ConflictPair[]): Set<string> {
  const set = new Set<string>();
  for (const c of conflicts) {
    const key = [c.firmAId, c.firmBId].sort().join("__");
    set.add(key);
  }
  return set;
}

export function hasConflict(firmIds: string[], conflictSet: Set<string>): boolean {
  for (let i = 0; i < firmIds.length; i++) {
    for (let j = i + 1; j < firmIds.length; j++) {
      const key = [firmIds[i], firmIds[j]].sort().join("__");
      if (conflictSet.has(key)) return true;
    }
  }
  return false;
}

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// Combination generation — choose k items from array
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

export function combinations<T>(arr: T[], k: number): T[][] {
  if (k === 0) return [[]];
  if (arr.length < k) return [];
  const [first, ...rest] = arr;
  const withFirst = combinations(rest, k - 1).map((combo) => [first, ...combo]);
  const withoutFirst = combinations(rest, k);
  return [...withFirst, ...withoutFirst];
}

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// Main entry point
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

export interface ScorerInput {
  capabilityMix: CapabilityMix;       // e.g. { Consultancy: 1, Infrastructure: 1, Nuclear: 1 }
  lockedFirmIds: string[];             // always included; fills required slots first
  weights: ScoringWeights;            // four sliders 0–100; normalised internally
  firms: Firm[];
  conflicts: ConflictPair[];
  partnerships: PartnershipPair[];
  ownership: OwnershipRecord[];
  riskFlags: RiskFlag[];
  procurement: ProcurementRecord[];
}

export interface ScorerResult {
  status: "ok" | "no_valid_team";
  reason?: string;                    // populated when status === "no_valid_team"
  recommendations: TeamRecommendation[]; // ≤3 teams, ranked highest score first
}

export function scoreAndRankCombinations(input: ScorerInput): ScorerResult {
  const {
    capabilityMix, lockedFirmIds, weights,
    firms: allFirms, conflicts, partnerships, ownership, riskFlags, procurement,
  } = input;

  // Normalise weights to sum to 1.0 (guard against all-zero weights)
  const rawTotal = weights.contractRelevance + weights.ukOwnership + weights.partnerships + weights.riskFlags;
  const wTotal = rawTotal === 0 ? 4 : rawTotal; // equal-weight fallback
  const w = {
    contractRelevance: weights.contractRelevance / wTotal,
    ukOwnership:       weights.ukOwnership / wTotal,
    partnerships:      weights.partnerships / wTotal,
    riskFlags:         weights.riskFlags / wTotal,
  };

  // Build conflict exclusion set once — O(conflicts) not O(combinations × conflicts)
  const conflictSet = buildConflictSet(conflicts);

  // Expand capabilityMix into a required-slots list (e.g. ["Consultancy","Infrastructure","Nuclear"])
  const requiredSlots: string[] = [];
  for (const [tag, count] of Object.entries(capabilityMix)) {
    for (let i = 0; i < count; i++) requiredSlots.push(tag);
  }
  const teamSize = requiredSlots.length;

  // Identify candidate firms: locked firms always in, others must cover at least one required tag
  const lockedFirms = allFirms.filter((f) => lockedFirmIds.includes(f.id));
  const unlockedFirms = allFirms.filter((f) => !lockedFirmIds.includes(f.id));

  // Generate all combinations of size (teamSize - lockedFirms.length) from unlocked pool
  const slotsToFill = teamSize - lockedFirms.length;
  const candidateCombinations =
    slotsToFill <= 0
      ? [[]] // all slots filled by locked firms
      : combinations(unlockedFirms, slotsToFill);

  const scoredTeams: Array<{ team: Firm[]; score: number; breakdown: FirmScoreBreakdown[] }> = [];

  for (const combo of candidateCombinations) {
    const teamFirms = [...lockedFirms, ...combo];
    const teamIds = teamFirms.map((f) => f.id);

    // Hard exclusion — conflicted pairs never co-recommended (CONS-03 / D-02)
    if (hasConflict(teamIds, conflictSet)) continue;

    // Capability coverage check — team must collectively cover all required slots
    const teamTags = teamFirms.flatMap((f): string[] => JSON.parse(f.capabilityTags ?? "[]"));
    const coversAll = requiredSlots.every((slot) => teamTags.includes(slot));
    if (!coversAll) continue;

    // Score each firm across all four signals
    const breakdown: FirmScoreBreakdown[] = teamFirms.map((firm) => {
      const contractScore  = scoreContractRelevance(firm.id, procurement);
      const ownershipScore = scoreUkOwnership(firm.id, ownership);
      const riskScore      = scoreRiskFlags(firm.id, riskFlags);
      const capabilityScore = scoreCapabilityMatch(firm, requiredSlots);

      const firmTotal =
        contractScore  * w.contractRelevance +
        ownershipScore * w.ukOwnership +
        riskScore      * w.riskFlags +
        capabilityScore * 0; // capability is a gate (coversAll), not a differentiating signal

      return {
        firmId: firm.id,
        firmName: firm.canonicalName,
        signalScores: {
          contractRelevance: contractScore,
          ukOwnership: ownershipScore,
          riskFlags: riskScore,
          capabilityMatch: capabilityScore,
        },
        weightedTotal: firmTotal,
        ownershipRecord: ownership.find((o) => o.firmId === firm.id) ?? null,
        riskFlagCount: riskFlags.filter((r) => r.firmId === firm.id).length,
        capabilityTags: JSON.parse(firm.capabilityTags ?? "[]"),
      };
    });

    // Team score = mean of firm weighted totals + partnership bonus
    const meanFirmScore = breakdown.reduce((s, b) => s + b.weightedTotal, 0) / breakdown.length;
    const partnershipBonus = teamHasPriorPartnerships(teamIds, partnerships)
      ? w.partnerships  // bonus = full weight for partnerships signal
      : 0;
    const teamScore = meanFirmScore + partnershipBonus;

    scoredTeams.push({ team: teamFirms, score: teamScore, breakdown });
  }

  if (scoredTeams.length === 0) {
    return {
      status: "no_valid_team",
      reason: "No valid combination found: all combinations either contain conflicted pairs or do not cover the requested capability mix.",
      recommendations: [],
    };
  }

  // Sort descending by score; tie-break alphabetically by concatenated firm names (deterministic)
  scoredTeams.sort((a, b) => {
    if (b.score !== a.score) return b.score - a.score;
    const nameA = a.team.map((f) => f.canonicalName).sort().join("|");
    const nameB = b.team.map((f) => f.canonicalName).sort().join("|");
    return nameA.localeCompare(nameB);
  });

  return {
    status: "ok",
    recommendations: scoredTeams.slice(0, 3).map((s, idx) => ({
      rank: idx + 1,
      score: s.score,
      firms: s.breakdown,
      hasPriorPartnerships: teamHasPriorPartnerships(s.team.map((f) => f.id), partnerships),
    })),
  };
}
```

**Tool Use:** None. Phase 5 has no LLM tool calls. The only external I/O is Drizzle ORM reads from Postgres (already established in Phase 1–4 patterns).

**State Management:** The scorer is stateless. All inputs are loaded from Postgres per request in `route.ts` and passed to the pure-function scorer. No in-memory state, no caching layer in Phase 5 (request volume is low — a single user running recommendations against 12 firms). If team-size grows significantly in v2, add `unstable_cache` from Next.js around the DB reads, not inside the scorer.

**Context Window Strategy:** Not applicable — no LLM in Phase 5. The only "context" concern is the number of firm combinations: for n=12 firms choosing k=3, there are C(12,3) = 220 combinations, each evaluated in microseconds. No pagination or batching needed at this scale.

## 4b. Deterministic System Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Adapted from the AI Systems Best Practices template for a deterministic rules-based TypeScript scorer (Phase 5 has no LLM). These are the cross-cutting engineering patterns for a DB-backed combinatorial decision system.

### 4b.1 Structured I/O Typing with Zod 4

Phase 5 uses Zod 4 (already in the project at `^4.4.3`) exactly where an AI system would use Pydantic: to validate the API boundary and make the output contract explicit and verifiable.

Define schemas in `src/lib/recommend/schemas.ts` with no Next.js imports so they are importable in tests without mocking:

```typescript
// src/lib/recommend/schemas.ts

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// Response schema — the shape the UI depends on
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  firmId:    z.string().uuid(),
  firmName:  z.string(),
  signalScores: z.object({
    contractRelevance: z.number().min(0).max(1),
    ukOwnership:       z.number().min(0).max(1),
    riskFlags:         z.number().min(0).max(1),
    capabilityMatch:   z.number().min(0).max(1),
  }),
  weightedTotal:    z.number().min(0),
  ownershipRecord:  z.any().nullable(),  // OwnershipRecord | null
  riskFlagCount:    z.number().int().min(0),

});

  rank:                 z.number().int().min(1).max(3),
  score:                z.number().min(0),
  firms:                z.array(FirmScoreBreakdownSchema),
  hasPriorPartnerships: z.boolean(),
});

export const RecommendResponseSchema = z.discriminatedUnion("status", [
  z.object({
    status:          z.literal("ok"),
    recommendations: z.array(TeamRecommendationSchema).min(1).max(3),
  }),
  z.object({
    status:  z.literal("no_valid_team"),
    reason:  z.string(),
    recommendations: z.array(TeamRecommendationSchema).length(0),

]);

// Inferred types — use these throughout the scorer and UI rather than re-declaring
export type CapabilityMix      = z.infer<typeof CapabilityMixSchema>;
export type ScoringWeights     = z.infer<typeof ScoringWeightsSchema>;
export type RecommendRequest   = z.infer<typeof RecommendRequestSchema>;
export type FirmScoreBreakdown = z.infer<typeof FirmScoreBreakdownSchema>;
export type TeamRecommendation = z.infer<typeof TeamRecommendationSchema>;
export type RecommendResponse  = z.infer<typeof RecommendResponseSchema>;
```

**Validation strategy for the route handler:**

- Use `.safeParse()` on the request body — never `.parse()` on untrusted input (throws, which Next.js turns into a 500).
- Use `.parse()` on the scorer's output (you control it; a parse failure here is a programming error you want thrown).
- The `z.discriminatedUnion("status", [...])` on the response schema means the UI can safely switch on `response.status` and get full type narrowing without a runtime check.

**No retry logic needed.** Unlike an LLM call, the scorer is deterministic: if it returns a valid TypeScript object that fails `RecommendResponseSchema.parse()`, the bug is in `scorer.ts`, not in the LLM's output. Fix the code, do not retry.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

The API route is `async` throughout, and all DB reads run in `Promise.all()` for parallel execution. The scorer itself is synchronous — a pure function that takes plain objects. This is the correct design split: async I/O at the boundary, sync computation at the core.

```typescript
// CORRECT: all six table reads run in parallel (~1 round-trip to Postgres)
const [allFirms, allConflicts, allPartnerships, allOwnership, allRiskFlags, allProcurement] =
  await Promise.all([
    db.select().from(firms),
    db.select().from(conflictPairs),
    db.select().from(partnershipPairs),
    db.select().from(ownershipRecords),
    db.select().from(riskFlags),
    db.select().from(procurementRecords),
  ]);

// WRONG: sequential awaits — 6× the latency for the same result
const allFirms      = await db.select().from(firms);
const allConflicts  = await db.select().from(conflictPairs);
// ... and so on
```

**The one common mistake in this stack:** calling `db.select()` inside a loop (e.g., inside the combination evaluator to load a firm's signals per combination). This produces O(combinations × tables) DB round-trips. Load all signals into memory once before the loop starts, then pass them to the pure-function scorer as plain arrays.

**Streaming:** Not applicable here. The scorer returns a small typed JSON object (≤3 teams × ≤5 firms of signal breakdowns). Stream only if you add a prose narrative layer via Claude in a future phase — never stream a deterministic JSON response.

### 4b.4 Immutable Score Objects

Score breakdowns are the audit trail for CONS-02 (explainability). Treat them as immutable records once computed: never mutate `signalScores` after creation, and never aggregate scores by averaging over a mutable accumulator. Compute each signal independently and compose:

```typescript
// CORRECT: compose from independent signal scorers, freeze result
const breakdown = Object.freeze({
  firmId: firm.id,
  firmName: firm.canonicalName,
  signalScores: Object.freeze({
    contractRelevance: scoreContractRelevance(firm.id, procurement),
    ukOwnership:       scoreUkOwnership(firm.id, ownership),
    riskFlags:         scoreRiskFlags(firm.id, riskFlags),
    capabilityMatch:   scoreCapabilityMatch(firm, requiredSlots),
  }),
  // ...
});

// WRONG: accumulate into a mutable object inside the combination loop
let total = 0;
total += contractScore;  // obscures which signal contributed what — breaks explainability
```

The UI reads `signalScores` per firm to render the per-signal breakdown panel required by CONS-02. If the scores are computed via mutable accumulation, the per-signal values cannot be recovered.

### 4b.5 Latency and Cost Budget

**Latency:** The full request lifecycle at 12 firms is:

| Step | Estimated latency |
|------|------------------|
| Zod parse of request body | < 1ms |
| 6× parallel Drizzle reads (local Postgres) | 5–20ms |
| `scoreAndRankCombinations()` at C(12,3) = 220 combos | < 1ms |
| Zod parse of response | < 1ms |
| **Total** | **~10–25ms per request** |

No caching, streaming, or batching is needed at this scale. The bottleneck is the Postgres round-trip, not the algorithm.

**Cost:** Zero. No LLM calls in Phase 5. All computation is in-process TypeScript.

**Scaling thresholds to keep in mind for v2:**
- At ~100 firms, C(100,3) = 161,700 combinations. The scorer is still fast (~10ms), but the DB reads become the bottleneck if Postgres is remote. Add `unstable_cache` from Next.js on the DB read layer at that point — the signal tables change only when a firm is refreshed.
- At ~500 firms, C(500,3) = 20,708,500 combinations. Switch from exhaustive enumeration to a greedy or beam-search approach. Do not reach for an LLM — a greedy scorer with constraint satisfaction still satisfies CONS-02 and CONS-03.
- The conflict exclusion `Set` lookup is O(1) regardless of firm count. Do not replace it with a database query inside the loop.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> **No LLM in the recommendation path → no LLM-judge evals.** This is a deterministic rules-based scorer, so every dimension is verifiable with code-based assertions. The "eval suite" is a Vitest unit + integration test suite run against pure functions and the seeded database. Determinism is the asset: the same input must always produce byte-identical output, which makes golden-file (snapshot) testing the natural fit for regression detection. The eval dimensions below are derived directly from the domain rubric ingredients in Section 1b — each maps to a `describe()` block in the test suite.

### Dimensions

| # | Dimension | Rubric (PASS / FAIL) | Measurement | Priority | Source (Section 1b) |
|---|-----------|----------------------|-------------|----------|---------------------|
| E1 | **Conflict-exclusion completeness** | **PASS:** No conflicted firm pair (audit or competitor) ever appears together in any returned recommendation, across all valid combinations — and the exclusion is reported with the reason. **FAIL:** Any combination containing a pair present in `conflictPairs` is returned, or a conflicted combination is silently scored instead of skipped. | **Code** — exhaustive assertion: for every team in `recommendations`, assert no pair is in the conflict set; property-style test over the full 12-firm pool. | **Critical** | "Conflict-of-interest completeness" |
| E2 | **NSI / foreign-ownership flag surfacing** | **PASS:** Every recommended firm carries its `ownershipRecord` (UK status + ultimate parent country); a non-UK / unknown ultimate parent yields an `ukOwnership` signal score < 1.0 and the badge data is present in the breakdown — never suppressed regardless of weight configuration. **FAIL:** A foreign-owned firm is returned with `ownershipRecord: null` when a record exists, or the NSI badge data is absent because the ownership weight was set to 0. | **Code** — assert `ownershipRecord` is non-null for every recommended firm that has a DB record; assert flag data present even when `weights.ukOwnership === 0`. | **Critical** | "NSI / foreign-ownership transparency" |
| E3 | **Scoring correctness vs spec** | **PASS:** Each signal scorer returns the exact value the formula in §4 dictates for fixed fixtures (e.g. 3-of-5 recent awards → `contractRelevance === 0.6`; one High flag → `riskFlags === 1 - 3/3 === 0`); normalised weighted total matches a hand-computed expected value within floating-point tolerance (`toBeCloseTo`). **FAIL:** Any signal scorer or the weighted-sum aggregation deviates from the spec formula. | **Code** — exact / `toBeCloseTo` assertions per signal scorer + one fully hand-computed weighted-total case. | **Critical** | "Score explainability — per-signal auditability" |
| E4 | **Weight monotonicity** | **PASS:** When two conflict-free teams differ on exactly one signal, raising that signal's weight never ranks the weaker team above the stronger one; a firm dominating on all signals is always preferred. **FAIL:** Increasing a signal's weight inverts the expected ranking between two otherwise-equal teams. | **Code** — parameterised test sweeping one weight while holding fixtures fixed, asserting rank order is preserved. | **High** | "Score explainability" (defensibility of ranking) |
| E5 | **Tie-break determinism** | **PASS:** Running the scorer N times on identical input yields identical `recommendations` (same order, same scores); equally-scored teams are ordered by the documented tie-break (alphabetical by sorted concatenated firm names). **FAIL:** Output order varies across runs, or the tie-break is non-deterministic (e.g. relies on `Set`/`Object` insertion order or unsorted DB rows). | **Code** — run scorer 100× on the same fixture, assert deep-equality of all results; explicit tie fixture asserting documented ordering. | **Critical** | Determinism is the core CONS-02 promise |
| E6 | **Per-signal explainability / citation completeness** | **PASS:** Every firm in every recommendation has all four `signalScores` populated, a `weightedTotal`, `capabilityTags`, `riskFlagCount`, and (where a DB record exists) a non-null `ownershipRecord` traceable to a DB row id. **FAIL:** Any recommended firm has a missing signal score, a composite-only score with no breakdown, or surfaces an assertion (ownership/risk/teaming) with no underlying DB record reference. | **Code** — `RecommendResponseSchema.parse()` on output + assertion that each `FirmScoreBreakdown` field is present and non-empty; cross-check ids resolve against fixture rows. | **High** | "Evidence provenance — sourced assertions only" + "per-signal auditability" |
| E7 | **Capability coverage — no silent gaps** | **PASS:** Every returned team collectively covers all requested capability tags; a request whose tags cannot be covered by any conflict-free team returns `status: "no_valid_team"` with a reason, not a partially-covering team. **FAIL:** A returned team is missing a requested tag, or a partial-match team is returned as if it covered the mix. | **Code** — assert `requiredSlots.every(tag => teamTags.includes(tag))` for every recommendation; negative test for an uncoverable mix. | **High** | "Capability coverage — no silent gaps" |
| E8 | **No-valid-team handling** | **PASS:** When no combination satisfies coverage + conflict constraints, the scorer returns `{ status: "no_valid_team", reason: <non-empty string>, recommendations: [] }` — an explicit, typed, reasoned state. **FAIL:** An empty `recommendations: []` is returned with `status: "ok"`, or no `reason`, or the route 500s. | **Code** — fixture engineered so all combos are conflicted/uncoverable; assert exact status + non-empty reason + empty array; `RecommendResponseSchema` discriminated-union narrowing holds. | **High** | Failure mode #4 (Section 1) + RES-05 invariant |
| E9 | **Seeded 12×7 end-to-end run (acceptance gate)** | **PASS:** Against the fully-seeded database (12 firms, 7 topics, Phase 1–4 data present), `POST /api/recommend` with the default 1+1+1 mix returns `status: "ok"` with 1 primary + up to 2 alternatives, each fully populated and schema-valid, and the `/report` page renders the result with no runtime error. **FAIL:** The seeded run errors, returns `no_valid_team` for the default mix, returns schema-invalid output, or `/report` fails to render. | **Code (integration)** + **Human** sign-off on the rendered `/report` page by the product owner. | **Critical** | CONS-05 acceptance gate; ROADMAP success criterion #5 |
| E10 | **Stale-data awareness (advisory)** | **PASS:** When any firm in a returned team has a `lastResearchedAt` older than the staleness threshold (or null), the response/UI surfaces a staleness indicator so the user knows conflict/ownership data may be out of date. **FAIL:** Stale or never-researched firms are recommended with no staleness signal, masking the "stale conflict data" risk (Section 1b known failure mode #1). | **Code** — assert staleness flag computed correctly for fixtures with old/null timestamps; **Human** review of the indicator in `/report`. | **Medium** | Known failure mode #1 (stale conflict data) |

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** ~14 labelled scenarios to start — small, high-signal, one per eval dimension plus the seeded golden case (per the "10–20 high-quality examples" guidance). Expand only when a real recommendation surprises the product owner.

**Composition:**
- **Critical paths (3):** the default 1+1+1 mix on the seeded 12 firms (E9 golden case); a clean 3-firm team with no conflicts; a multi-slot mix (e.g. 2 Consultancy + 1 Nuclear).
- **Conflict edge cases (3):** one conflicted pair present (E1); all combinations conflicted → no valid team (E8); a conflict that must hold regardless of firm argument order.
- **Ownership / NSI cases (2):** a foreign-owned firm that must surface its NSI badge even with `ukOwnership` weight = 0 (E2); an "unknown ownership" firm scoring the 0.5 neutral.
- **Scoring / ranking cases (3):** hand-computed weighted-total fixture (E3); weight-monotonicity sweep (E4); an exact-tie fixture exercising the deterministic tie-break (E5).
- **Coverage / explainability cases (2):** an uncoverable capability mix (E7); a full-breakdown completeness assertion (E6).
- **Staleness case (1):** a team containing a firm with null / old `lastResearchedAt` (E10).

**Labeling:**
- **Product owner (Luke Latimer)** acts as domain-expert proxy (per Section 1b roles): confirms the expected winning team and ranking for the seeded golden case and signs off the E9 `/report` render. Each fixture encodes its expected output as the test assertion — the "label" is the hand-computed expected score/ranking committed in the test file.
- **Procurement-compliance lens (proxy):** the conflict-exclusion (E1) and NSI (E2) fixtures are labelled against the Procurement Act 2023 s.26 and NSI Act 2021 criteria captured in Section 1b — a conflicted pair must be excluded; a non-UK parent must surface a flag.
- Because outputs are deterministic, labels are exact values (not subjective scores), so no LLM-judge calibration is required.

## 6. Guardrails

> Guardrails for a deterministic local-first tool are **not** LLM content filters — there is no model output to moderate. They are (1) input validation at the API boundary and (2) data-integrity / staleness checks that prevent the scorer acting on bad or out-of-date inputs. The catastrophic-vs-quality test from `ai-evals.md` still applies: a guardrail runs on every request and intervenes; a flywheel metric runs as a batch (here, the Vitest suite) and feeds improvement.

### Online (Real-Time — runs on every `/api/recommend` request)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Zod request validation** | `RecommendRequestSchema.safeParse()` fails (malformed `capabilityMix`, out-of-range weight, non-UUID `lockedFirmId`, or all-zero capability mix). | **Block** — return `400` with `error.flatten()`; never run the scorer on unvalidated input. |
| **Zod response validation** | `RecommendResponseSchema.parse()` fails on the scorer's output (a programming error broke the contract). | **Block** — throw before responding; a contract violation is a bug to fix, not a result to ship (per §4b.1, no retry). |
| **Conflict-exclusion invariant (defence in depth)** | Any team about to be returned contains a pair in the conflict set (should be impossible given exclusion logic — this is a belt-and-braces assertion). | **Block** — drop the team / fail loud rather than ever surfacing a conflicted consortium. This is the highest-stakes failure mode (Section 1, #1). |
| **NSI flag non-suppression** | A recommended firm has an `ownershipRecord` in the DB but the breakdown would omit it, OR `weights.ukOwnership === 0`. | **Flag** — always attach `ownershipRecord` + NSI badge data to the breakdown regardless of weights; the weight controls ranking influence, never flag visibility (Section 1, #2). |
| **No-valid-team explicit status** | The scorer produces zero valid combinations. | **Flag** — return `{ status: "no_valid_team", reason }`; never an `ok` with an empty array (Section 1, #4). |
| **Stale-data advisory** | A recommended firm's `lastResearchedAt` is null or older than the staleness threshold. | **Flag** — surface a staleness indicator on the firm in the result so the user knows conflict/ownership data may be out of date (Section 1b known failure mode #1). Advisory, not blocking. |

### Offline (Flywheel — the Vitest suite as the batch quality gate)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Full eval-suite pass rate (E1–E9)** | Run `npm test` on every commit and as the pre-deploy gate (not sampled — the suite is fast enough to run in full). | Block the commit / deploy until green; investigate any newly-failing dimension before merge. |
| **Seeded golden-snapshot diff (E9)** | Snapshot of the seeded 12×7 recommendation compared on every run. | Any unintended diff fails CI → review whether a weight/formula/tie-break change was intentional before updating the snapshot with `-u`. |
| **Score-distribution sanity (manual review)** | After each Phase 1–4 data refresh that changes firm signals, re-run the seeded report and eyeball the top-3 teams. | If a re-research run flips the recommended team, confirm the underlying data change is real (not a name-match false positive — Section 1b failure mode #2) before trusting the new result. |
| **New real-world conflict / NSI case** | When the product owner encounters a real conflict or ownership case the tool got wrong. | Add it as a new fixture to the reference dataset and a regression test — grow the suite from real failures, not hypotheticals. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** **Structured `console.log` / `console.error` with JSON payloads** — no external tracing platform. This is a single-user, local-first Next.js app with a deterministic scorer; there are no token costs, no model latency, and no non-determinism for a tracing platform (Phoenix/Langfuse/LangSmith) to add value over structured logs. The server console (or a piped log file) is the observability surface for v1. Revisit only if/when the tool is deployed to a shared cloud environment.

Each `/api/recommend` invocation emits one structured log line:

```typescript
// src/app/api/recommend/route.ts — after the scorer returns
console.log(JSON.stringify({
  event: "recommend.request",
  ts: new Date().toISOString(),
  capabilityMix,                       // requested mix (no PII — firms are legal entities)
  lockedFirmCount: lockedFirmIds.length,
  weights,                             // normalised slider config
  firmsLoaded: allFirms.length,        // DB row counts loaded (sanity: expect 12 seeded)
  conflictsLoaded: allConflicts.length,
  combinationsEvaluated,               // count of combos generated/scored
  status: result.status,               // "ok" | "no_valid_team"
  recommendationCount: result.recommendations.length,
  staleFirmsInResult,                  // count of recommended firms with stale/null lastResearchedAt
  latencyMs,                           // wall-clock from request start to response
}));
// On a thrown contract/validation error, emit the same shape via console.error with event: "recommend.error".
```

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Result status mix** — ratio of `ok` vs `no_valid_team` over time; a spike in `no_valid_team` signals a data gap or an over-broad conflict registry.
2. **Combinations evaluated** — sanity-checks pool size and capability mix; an unexpected count flags a seeding or capability-tag problem.
3. **DB row counts loaded** — `firmsLoaded` / `conflictsLoaded` etc.; a sudden drop indicates a seeding or Phase 1–4 data regression.
4. **Stale firms in result** — count of recommended firms with null/old `lastResearchedAt`; the early-warning signal for the stale-conflict-data failure mode.
5. **Latency (ms)** — expected ~10–25ms per §4b.5; sustained growth flags a DB or combinatorial-scaling problem.

**Alert Thresholds (local-first — surface in console, no paging infra):**
- **Error log emitted** (`recommend.error`) → investigate immediately; a contract/validation failure is always a bug.
- `firmsLoaded === 0` or `< 12` on the seeded DB → seeding/DB regression; the recommender cannot function.
- `status === "no_valid_team"` for the **default 1+1+1 mix** → the E9 acceptance gate is effectively broken; investigate before trusting any report.
- `latencyMs > 250` (10× the §4b.5 budget) → DB round-trip or scaling regression.
- `staleFirmsInResult > 0` in a report being acted upon → advise a re-research before relying on the recommendation.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
Single-user volume makes full review trivial, so sampling is signal-based rather than rate-based — surface a log/result for human (product-owner) review when **any** of: `status === "no_valid_team"`, an error was logged, `staleFirmsInResult > 0`, or the seeded golden snapshot changed since the last run. These are the interactions most likely to indicate a data or logic problem; clean `ok` runs with no stale firms need no manual review.
