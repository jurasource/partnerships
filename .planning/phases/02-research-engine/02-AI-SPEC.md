## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Bounded Structured Extraction / Synthesis Loop (tool-augmented, citation-grounded research worker)

**Description:**
A per-(firm × topic) research worker that assembles a grounding context block from cached UK structured data (Companies House profile, PSC nationality, OCDS procurement records), issues 1–2 Tavily web searches per topic, then calls Claude via `generateObject` to synthesise Zod-validated findings with mandatory citations. Every finding is tagged by provenance (HIGH = structured-API fact; MEDIUM = AI-derived with cited Tavily URL). LOW-confidence findings (AI-derived, no verifiable source) are rejected and replaced with an explicit "not found" sentinel. The worker runs all 7 topics sequentially inside a single fire-and-forget Next.js API route, updating job status for poll-based progress visibility. Users are bid/consortium planners evaluating UK firms for civil nuclear and defence programmes — "good" means every stored finding is attributable, absent findings are explicit, and nothing is silently missing.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
<!-- The 3-5 behaviors that absolutely cannot go wrong in this system -->
1. **Unsourced claim stored as fact** — a finding is written to the `findings` table without a valid `sourceUrl`, `sourceTitle`, or `rawSnippet`. Users cannot verify it, defeating the core value proposition.
2. **Silent absence** — a topic produces no findings and no "not found" sentinel. The UI shows nothing; the user cannot distinguish "not researched" from "nothing found". Decision-makers act on missing information.
3. **Provenance collapse** — structured-API facts (HIGH confidence) are stored with the same confidence tag as AI-derived claims (MEDIUM), or vice versa. Users lose the ability to distinguish verified UK registry data from AI narrative.
4. **Hallucinated citation** — the AI generates a plausible-sounding `sourceUrl` that does not correspond to the retrieved Tavily content. The citation passes schema validation but the link is fabricated or misattributed.
5. **Job status freeze** — a research job transitions to `running` and never updates to `done` or `failed`. The UI shows infinite progress; the user cannot re-trigger.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** UK public procurement intelligence — civil nuclear and defence bid/consortium planning

**User Population:** Bid managers, business development leads, and consortium planners at firms competing for UK civil nuclear and defence programme contracts (Sellafield, NDA, MOD, DSTL, AWE). They use findings to decide which firms to partner with, which to treat as competitors, and which to exclude on ownership, capability, or risk grounds — before submitting a consortium bid or expressing interest in a framework.

**Stakes Level:** Critical

**Output Consequence:** Findings are acted on directly in consortium formation decisions. A bid manager reading a "no ownership concerns" finding for a potential partner may proceed without further checks. A missed PSC nationality flag, a stale filing cited as current, or a contract attributed to the wrong entity can result in: selecting a partner that fails security vetting and forces a last-minute consortium restructure; submitting a bid that is disqualified for undisclosed foreign ownership; or, in the worst case, reputational and programme damage on a multi-billion-pound contract. The downstream cost of a false negative (missing a red flag) or a false positive (raising a spurious concern that eliminates a good partner) is measured in contract value, not user satisfaction.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

---

**Dimension: Source currency — is this still true today?**

Good (bid manager accepts): Finding states "As of [date], Companies House PSC register shows three persons with significant control; two are UK nationals (DOB: 19XX); one is a German national (DOB: 19XX). Filing date: [date]." The date is within 12 months and the filing matches the current CH register.

Bad (bid manager flags): Finding states "The firm is UK-owned" without citing which filing date or PSC register version was consulted. A firm can change ownership without triggering a new AI search; a 2021 filing treated as current is a silent staleness failure.

Stakes: Critical

Source: Companies House filing obligations (annual confirmation statement); practitioner knowledge — PSC registers are point-in-time snapshots and ownership structures in defence supply chains change through M&A, private equity restructuring, and TUPE-related entity creation.

---

**Dimension: Entity disambiguation — is this finding about the right legal entity?**

Good (bid manager accepts): Finding references "Mott MacDonald Limited (Companies House number 01243967)" explicitly, distinguishing it from Mott MacDonald Group Limited and other Mott MacDonald entities. Procurement records are matched by Companies House number, not name alone.

Bad (bid manager flags): Finding reports a contract win for "Atkins" without specifying whether that is SNC-Lavalin Atkins Limited, Atkins Limited, or WS Atkins plc (a dissolved predecessor). Contract attributed to a subsidiary treated as the parent, or to a firm with a similar trading name, cannot be acted on safely.

Stakes: Critical

Source: REQUIREMENTS.md DATA-04 — "Firms are matched to procurement records by Companies House number (not name alone)"; practitioner knowledge — the nuclear and defence supply chain contains many firms with shared group branding across legally distinct entities (AECOM, Jacobs, Serco, Atkins/AtkinsRealis, Mott MacDonald).

---

**Dimension: Provenance separation — can I tell a verified fact from an AI inference?**

Good (bid manager accepts): Finding shows confidence = HIGH for "UK-incorporated; two PSCs, both UK nationals (CH filing 2024-11-01)" and a separate confidence = MEDIUM finding for "Firm has publicly stated interest in the Sizewell C supply chain (source: Nuclear Industry Association press release, Jan 2025, URL cited)." The user can immediately see which claim requires independent verification.

Bad (bid manager flags): A finding that mixes "The firm holds £45m of Sellafield contracts (CH/OCDS verified)" and "The firm is considered a leading nuclear decommissioning specialist (AI narrative)" in the same summary field with no provenance distinction. The user cannot tell what to trust without re-checking the sources themselves.

Stakes: High

Source: REQUIREMENTS.md RES-04; ai-evals.md — context faithfulness and hallucination detection; practitioner knowledge — due diligence analysts are trained to distinguish primary source data from secondary commentary and treat them differently in risk reports.

---

**Dimension: Explicit absence — "nothing found" is a finding, not a gap**

Good (bid manager accepts): Finding for topic "Reputational Risk" reads: "Not found: No adverse media, regulatory censure, or reported contract failures identified for [firm] in Tavily search results dated [date]. This does not constitute a clearance — absence of indexed adverse media is not equivalent to a clean record." The user knows the topic was researched and the scope of what was searched.

Bad (bid manager flags): The reputational risk topic has no row in the findings table and no entry in the UI. The bid manager cannot tell if research ran and found nothing, if research failed partway through, or if the topic was never run. In a high-stakes bid review, a blank is treated as a system error, not as a clean finding.

Stakes: High

Source: REQUIREMENTS.md RES-05 — "Explicit 'not found' is a first-class output"; practitioner knowledge — due diligence reports in the sector always explicitly state scope of search and negative findings, because silence is read as omission, not clearance.

---

**Dimension: Citation precision — can I verify this in under 60 seconds?**

Good (bid manager accepts): Finding states: rawSnippet = verbatim text from the Tavily result; sourceUrl = the exact URL returned by Tavily; sourceTitle = the page title. Opening the URL and searching for the snippet text takes under a minute and confirms the claim.

Bad (bid manager flags): sourceUrl is a homepage (e.g. "https://www.firmname.co.uk") rather than the specific page where the claim appears. rawSnippet is a paraphrase rather than a verbatim excerpt. The user cannot verify the claim without re-doing the search themselves — defeating the system's core value.

Stakes: High

Source: REQUIREMENTS.md RES-03 — "mandatory source citations with raw retrieved snippet"; ai-evals.md — hallucination detection; practitioner knowledge — bid managers and due diligence analysts routinely audit AI-generated summaries against cited sources before acting on them.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

**1. Stale ownership cited as current.** Companies House PSC filings are point-in-time. A firm acquired by a foreign parent in 2023 may still appear UK-owned in cached data or in web search results referencing older profiles. The system cites the filing without noting its date or flagging that it may not reflect the current ownership structure. A bid manager uses this to clear a firm on NSI Act grounds — only to discover during security vetting that the firm was acquired by a non-UK entity and requires mandatory NSI notification.

**2. Entity confusion across a corporate group.** Many firms in the UK nuclear/defence supply chain operate under shared branding across legally distinct subsidiaries (e.g. "Jacobs" could be Jacobs UK Limited, Jacobs Clean Energy Limited, or CH2M Hill (now Jacobs) entities). A procurement record attributed to a parent or sibling entity is misread as relevant to the firm being researched. The bid manager concludes the firm has stronger contract history in the sector than it does.

**3. Missing PSC nationality issue that blocks security clearance.** The AI narrative summarises ownership as "primarily UK-controlled" because the majority of PSCs are UK nationals — but a minority non-UK PSC with a role in technical operations or data access may create a SC/DV vetting problem that prevents programme staff from obtaining clearance. The system does not flag minority PSC nationalities individually; only the majority finding is surfaced.

**4. Contract from a different division or historical entity treated as directly relevant.** A firm has a long procurement record under a previous company name (pre-acquisition) or under a subsidiary that no longer exists. The AI includes these in the "UK contracts held" finding without noting the corporate structure change. The bid manager overestimates the firm's current programme relationship and track record.

**5. Web search result about a different firm with a similar name.** The system issues a Tavily search for "[Firm Name] nuclear contracts UK" and retrieves results about a different, similarly-named firm — especially plausible for firms with common words in their name (e.g. "Nuclear Management Partners" vs. "Nuclear Management Services"). The finding cites the correct-looking URL but the content is about the wrong entity.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**National Security and Investment Act 2021 (NSI Act) — Mandatory:** Civil nuclear and defence are among the 17 sensitive sectors where acquisitions crossing share/voting-right thresholds (25%, 50%, 75%) require mandatory government notification and approval before completing. Penalty for non-compliance: up to 5% of global turnover or £10m, plus up to 5 years imprisonment for individuals. For consortium planning: a firm with a non-UK ultimate parent operating in these sectors may have existing NSI conditions attached to its UK operations that constrain what it can participate in. The research engine must surface ownership nationality prominently so planners can identify which firms need legal advice on NSI exposure before being named in a bid. (Reforms consulted on in 2025 are streamlining but not removing the mandatory notification regime.)

**Security Vetting (SC and DV Clearance):** UK government programme staff and supply chain personnel on civil nuclear and defence contracts are typically required to hold Security Check (SC) or Developed Vetting (DV) clearance. Non-UK nationals (and in some cases UK nationals with significant overseas ties) face additional scrutiny or are ineligible for certain clearance levels. A firm with multiple non-UK PSCs or a non-UK ultimate parent may face workforce restrictions that limit its ability to deliver programme obligations. The research engine should flag PSC nationality data (from Companies House) as a direct input to this assessment.

**ITAR / Export Control:** US International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) restrict transfer of controlled defence technology to non-US persons, including non-US employees of UK firms. For firms in the nuclear and defence supply chain with US-origin technology or US programme work, employing non-UK/non-US nationals in relevant roles creates compliance exposure. This is a secondary concern for the research engine (not directly surfaced by Companies House or Tavily) but relevant when a firm's ownership or workforce is flagged as primarily non-Western.

**Government Modern Slavery Act 2015:** UK government suppliers above the £36m turnover threshold are required to publish an annual Modern Slavery transparency statement. Non-compliance is a potential disqualifying factor in government procurement pre-qualification. This is a low-priority signal for the research engine but a valid reputational risk finding when a firm's compliance statement is absent or outdated.

**Companies House Filing Obligations:** UK-incorporated firms must file annual confirmation statements and accounts. Overdue or dormant filings can indicate a firm is inactive, in financial distress, or has restructured. The research engine surfaces CH status (active/dissolved/dormant) as a HIGH-confidence fact — this is a primary check for bid managers before engaging a firm.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Vercel AI SDK 5.x (`ai` package) with `@ai-sdk/anthropic` provider

**Version:** ai@5.x (latest stable), @ai-sdk/anthropic@1.x

**Rationale:**
`generateObject` + Zod is the exact primitive this system needs: schema-validated structured output where the schema itself enforces the citation invariant (non-empty `sourceUrl`/`sourceTitle`/`rawSnippet`). The worker runs inside a Next.js 15 App Router API route — the AI SDK is native TypeScript, zero-dependency overhead, and is already the locked stack choice in CLAUDE.md. Provider-agnostic design (swapping to OpenAI/Gemini is a one-line change) means no vendor lock-in despite Anthropic being the default. The research loop is linear and deterministic; no agent orchestration runtime (LangGraph, CrewAI) is needed.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain.js | Abstraction overhead for a well-defined loop; chains/runnables add complexity with no benefit for a linear synthesis worker |
| LangGraph.js | Built for stateful branching workflows with checkpointing; this phase is linear and explicitly defers per-topic retry |
| Claude Agent SDK | Code-aware autonomous agent framework — wrong shape for bounded structured extraction; would add Claude-only lock-in |
| LlamaIndex.TS | RAG/retrieval-first; findings are cached structured records, not a semantic corpus. CLAUDE.md: "No vector DB / RAG infra for v1" |
| CrewAI / AutoGen / Google ADK | Python-only (CrewAI, AutoGen) or Gemini-oriented (ADK), multi-agent — none fit a single TypeScript synthesis loop |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — `@ai-sdk/anthropic` is the default provider but the AI SDK's provider-agnostic interface means swapping to another provider is a one-line change. Anthropic Claude is preferred for long-context synthesis and citation behaviour.

### Entry Point Pattern

Minimal working example for this system type: `generateText` with `Output.object()` returning a Zod-validated finding inside a Next.js App Router Route Handler.

```typescript
// src/app/api/research/run/route.ts
import { generateText, Output } from 'ai';
import { anthropic } from '@ai-sdk/anthropic';
import { z } from 'zod';

// Zod schema — the citation fields are non-optional by design (D-11)
const FindingSchema = z.object({
  summary:     z.string().describe('1-3 sentence synthesis of the finding'),
  confidence:  z.enum(['HIGH', 'MEDIUM']).describe('HIGH = structured API fact; MEDIUM = AI-derived with cited source'),
  sourceUrl:   z.string().min(1).describe('URL of the primary source; use "not-found" sentinel only when emitting absence'),
  sourceTitle: z.string().min(1).describe('Title of the primary source document or page'),
  rawSnippet:  z.string().min(1).describe('Verbatim excerpt or data from the source that supports the summary'),
});

export async function POST(req: Request) {
  const { firmId, jobId } = await req.json();

  // Build grounding context (Companies House + OCDS data — already cached)
  const groundingContext = await buildGroundingContext(firmId);

  // 1-2 Tavily searches per topic (D-10)
  const searchResults = await tavilyClient.search(`${firm.name} nuclear defence contracts UK`);

  const result = await generateText({
    model: anthropic('claude-sonnet-4-6'),
    system: RESEARCH_SYSTEM_PROMPT,           // see Section 4
    prompt: buildTopicPrompt(topic, groundingContext, searchResults),
    output: Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema }),
    maxOutputTokens: 512,                     // renamed from maxTokens in v5
    temperature: 0.1,                         // low for factual extraction
    maxRetries: 2,                            // SDK-level retry on transient provider errors
  });

  // result.output is typed as z.infer<typeof FindingSchema> — no manual parse needed
  const finding = result.output;

  await db.insert(findings).values({ firmId, topicId: topic.id, ...finding });

  return Response.json({ ok: true });
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `generateText` + `Output.object()` | Replaces v4 `generateObject`. Calls the model and validates the response against a Zod schema; throws `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError` on validation failure. Returns `result.output` as the typed object. | Every per-(firm × topic) synthesis call where a Zod-validated structured finding is required. |
| `anthropic('claude-sonnet-4-6')` | Creates a `LanguageModel` instance scoped to a specific Claude model. Reads `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` from env by default. Accepts `anthropic.messages()` / `anthropic.chat()` / `anthropic.languageModel()` as aliases. | Passed as the `model` parameter to `generateText`. Use `claude-haiku-4-5` for cheap routing/classification sub-tasks. |
| `Output.array({ schema })` | Like `Output.object()` but expects the model to return a JSON array matching the element schema. | Use when asking the model to emit multiple findings for a topic in one call rather than one at a time. |
| `tool()` helper + `inputSchema` | Defines a callable tool. In v5 the parameter key is `inputSchema` (not `parameters` — that's a v4 name). Tool results flow back to the model via `stopWhen: stepCountIs(N)`. | Tavily search *could* be wrapped as a tool here; D-02 instead wraps it imperatively and passes results as grounding context. Tool wrapping is appropriate if you want the model to decide when to search. |
| `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError` | Thrown when the model fails to produce a response conforming to the Zod schema after `maxRetries`. Exposes `.text` (raw model output), `.usage`, and `.cause`. | Catch this to write a "not-found" sentinel rather than letting the job crash (see Section 4b.1). |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Using `generateObject` (v4 API)** — This function does not exist in AI SDK v5. The migration guide explicitly replaces it with `generateText({ output: Output.object({ schema }) })`. Any autocomplete, LLM suggestion, or tutorial showing `import { generateObject } from 'ai'` is targeting v4 and will produce a compile error.

2. **Passing `maxTokens` instead of `maxOutputTokens`** — Renamed in v5. Passing the old name silently does nothing (TypeScript will catch it, but only if `strict` mode is on and the type definitions are current). Always use `maxOutputTokens` in v5 and confirm in the function signature.

3. **Assuming `result.output` is always populated** — `result.output` is `undefined` when no `output` parameter is passed, or when the model fails schema validation and `maxRetries` is exhausted. Always guard with `if (!result.output)` or catch `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError` before writing to the database.

4. **Leaving `maxOutputTokens` unbounded in production** — Without an explicit cap, a pathological response will run to the provider's context limit and incur unexpected cost. For structured extraction where outputs are small (a 3-field finding), 512 tokens is sufficient. Set it explicitly on every call.

5. **Mixing v4 `parameters` with v5 `inputSchema` in tool definitions** — Tool definitions in v5 use `inputSchema`, not `parameters`. Using the old key compiles (it becomes an extra unknown property) but the schema is not passed to the model, so the model has no type information for the tool's arguments. Tool calls will be untyped or fail.

6. **Calling `generateText` inside a synchronous Next.js Route Handler body** — Route Handlers in Next.js App Router are `async` by default when exported, but `generateText` returns a Promise. Forgetting `await` means the response is returned before the AI call completes; the job status is never updated and the route returns an empty body.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

| Parameter | Value | Reason |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| `model` | `anthropic('claude-sonnet-4-6')` | Primary synthesis model: strong long-context grounding, reliable citation behaviour for multi-source firm analysis |
| `model` (routing sub-tasks) | `anthropic('claude-haiku-4-5')` | Optional: cheaper model for topic query shaping, not required for v1 given small per-topic prompt size |
| `maxOutputTokens` | `512` | Structured extraction — a single finding (summary + 3 citation fields) fits comfortably in 512 tokens. Caps cost per call. |
| `temperature` | `0.1` | Near-deterministic: factual extraction and citation from provided sources, not creative generation |
| `maxRetries` | `2` | SDK-level retry covers transient provider/network errors. Schema validation failures are caught separately (see 4b.1). |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | `.env.local` | Never hardcoded. Next.js reads `process.env.ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` automatically in server-side code. |

**Core Pattern:**

The research worker (`src/lib/research/worker.ts`) runs all 7 topics sequentially for one firm. For each topic it:

1. Assembles a grounding context block from cached Companies House + OCDS data (`buildGroundingContext`)
2. Issues 1–2 Tavily searches (`WebSearchClient.search`) and collects results
3. Calls `generateText` with `Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema })` to synthesise a Zod-validated finding
4. Inspects `result.output.confidence`:
   - `HIGH` — structured API data (write directly)
   - `MEDIUM` — AI-derived with a cited Tavily URL (write if `sourceUrl` is non-empty and matches a retrieved result URL)
   - If output is `undefined` or `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError` is thrown — write a "not-found" sentinel (D-09, D-11)
5. Inserts the finding into the `findings` table via Drizzle

```typescript
// src/lib/research/worker.ts
import { generateText, Output, AI_NoObjectGeneratedError } from 'ai';
import { anthropic } from '@ai-sdk/anthropic';
import { db } from '@/db';
import { findings } from '@/db/schema';
import { FindingSchema, NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL } from './schemas';
import { buildGroundingContext } from './grounding';
import { buildTopicPrompt, RESEARCH_SYSTEM_PROMPT } from './prompts';
import type { WebSearchClient } from '@/lib/search/types';

export async function runResearchWorker(
  firmId: number,
  jobId: string,
  searchClient: WebSearchClient,
): Promise<void> {
  const groundingContext = await buildGroundingContext(firmId);

  for (const topic of RESEARCH_TOPICS) {
    // D-10: 1-2 Tavily searches per topic
    const searchResults = await searchClient.search(
      buildSearchQuery(groundingContext.firmName, topic),
    );

    let finding: z.infer<typeof FindingSchema>;

    try {
      const result = await generateText({
        model: anthropic('claude-sonnet-4-6'),
        system: RESEARCH_SYSTEM_PROMPT,
        prompt: buildTopicPrompt(topic, groundingContext, searchResults),
        output: Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema }),
        maxOutputTokens: 512,
        temperature: 0.1,
        maxRetries: 2,
      });

      if (!result.output) {
        // Model returned text but schema validation failed after retries
        finding = NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topic.id, 'Schema validation failed — no structured output produced');
      } else {
        finding = result.output;
      }
    } catch (err) {
      if (err instanceof AI_NoObjectGeneratedError) {
        // Log raw output for debugging; write sentinel so topic is not silently absent
        console.error(`[research] NoObject for ${firmId}/${topic.id}:`, err.text, err.cause);
        finding = NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topic.id, 'Model failed to produce structured output');
      } else {
        throw err; // re-throw — outer handler marks job failed (D-05)
      }
    }

    await db.insert(findings).values({
      firmId,
      topicId:     topic.id,
      summary:     finding.summary,
      confidence:  finding.confidence,
      sourceUrl:   finding.sourceUrl,
      sourceTitle: finding.sourceTitle,
      rawSnippet:  finding.rawSnippet,
      createdAt:   new Date(),
    });
  }
}
```

**Tool Use:**

Tavily is called imperatively (not as a model tool) per D-02 and D-08. This is the correct pattern for this system: the number of searches is fixed (1–2 per topic), results are injected into the prompt as grounding context, and the model's job is synthesis not search orchestration.

The `WebSearchClient` interface (D-02) keeps the call site decoupled from Tavily:

```typescript
// src/lib/search/types.ts
export interface WebSearchResult {
  url:     string;
  title:   string;
  snippet: string;
}

export interface WebSearchClient {
  search(query: string, maxResults?: number): Promise<WebSearchResult[]>;
}
```

```typescript
// src/lib/search/tavily.ts
import { WebSearchClient, WebSearchResult } from './types';

export class TavilySearchClient implements WebSearchClient {
  constructor(private readonly apiKey = process.env.TAVILY_API_KEY!) {}

  async search(query: string, maxResults = 5): Promise<WebSearchResult[]> {
    const res = await fetch('https://api.tavily.com/search', {
      method: 'POST',
      headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
      body: JSON.stringify({ api_key: this.apiKey, query, max_results: maxResults }),
    });
    const data = await res.json();
    return (data.results ?? []).map((r: any) => ({
      url:     r.url,
      title:   r.title,
      snippet: r.content,
    }));
  }
}
```

If in a future phase the model should dynamically decide when to search, wrap Tavily as an AI SDK tool using the `tool()` helper with `inputSchema: z.object({ query: z.string() })` and an `execute` function.

**State Management:**

State is managed entirely via the `jobs` and `findings` tables in SQLite (Drizzle). The AI SDK itself is stateless between calls — no session or conversation memory is maintained.

- `jobs.status` progresses: `pending` → `running` → `done` | `failed` (D-03)
- `updateJobStatus(jobId, 'running')` is called once at the start of the worker
- `updateJobStatus(jobId, 'done')` is called after all 7 topics complete
- On any unhandled error, the outer `POST /api/research/run` handler calls `updateJobStatus(jobId, 'failed', errorMessage)` (D-05)
- Progress ("N/7 topics complete") is derived at read time: `SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT topicId) FROM findings WHERE firmId = ? AND createdAt > job.startedAt` — no extra column needed (D-06)
- `firms.lastResearchedAt` is updated to `NOW()` when job transitions to `done` (D-07)

**Context Window Strategy:**

The grounding context block passed to `buildTopicPrompt` must be bounded to prevent crowding the Tavily snippets. Apply these limits at assembly time in `buildGroundingContext`:

- Companies House profile: always included as a compact block (~200 tokens): company status, SIC codes, incorporation country, PSC nationality summary (e.g. "3 of 4 PSCs are non-UK nationals")
- Procurement records: top 10 most recent records only (title, date, value, authority, source), trimmed to ~500 tokens total; older records dropped not summarised
- Tavily results: up to 5 results × ~150 tokens per snippet = ~750 tokens
- System prompt: ~300 tokens
- Total input budget: keep under 3,000 tokens per call, leaving claude-sonnet-4-6's 200k window extremely comfortable and keeping per-call cost low

If structured data for a firm is unusually large (e.g. hundreds of procurement records), select the top 10 by value descending, not truncate arbitrarily.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.
> All examples are TypeScript for AI SDK v5 + Zod v4.

### Structured Outputs with Zod

**Schema definition** — The `FindingSchema` enforces the citation invariant (D-11) at the type system level. Every field that the UI or database requires is `z.string().min(1)`, not `z.string().optional()`. The schema is the contract; the prompt is the instruction.

```typescript
// src/lib/research/schemas.ts
import { z } from 'zod';

export const FindingSchema = z.object({
  summary: z
    .string()
    .min(10)
    .describe('1-3 sentence synthesis of the finding, grounded in the provided sources'),
  confidence: z
    .enum(['HIGH', 'MEDIUM'])
    .describe(
      'HIGH = fact drawn directly from UK structured API data (Companies House / Contracts Finder / FTS). ' +
      'MEDIUM = AI-derived finding with a cited Tavily source URL. ' +
      'Never emit LOW — emit the not-found sentinel instead.',
    ),
  sourceUrl: z
    .string()
    .min(1)
    .describe('URL of the primary source. Use "not-found" only when emitting the absence sentinel.'),
  sourceTitle: z
    .string()
    .min(1)
    .describe('Human-readable title of the source document or web page'),
  rawSnippet: z
    .string()
    .min(1)
    .describe('Verbatim excerpt or data from the source that directly supports the summary'),
});

export type Finding = z.infer<typeof FindingSchema>;

// Sentinel value for explicit absence (D-11) — avoids silent missing rows
export function NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topicId: string, reason: string): Finding {
  return {
    summary:     `Not found: ${reason}`,
    confidence:  'HIGH',   // sentinel is a definitive structured fact: "we looked and found nothing"
    sourceUrl:   'not-found',
    sourceTitle: 'Research worker',
    rawSnippet:  `No verifiable source found for topic "${topicId}". Reason: ${reason}`,
  };
}
```

**Framework integration** — AI SDK v5 uses `Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema })` passed to `generateText`. The SDK validates `result.output` against the schema before returning. If validation fails, it retries up to `maxRetries` times, then throws `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError`.

```typescript
import { generateText, Output, AI_NoObjectGeneratedError } from 'ai';
import { anthropic } from '@ai-sdk/anthropic';
import { FindingSchema, NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL } from './schemas';

async function synthesiseFinding(
  topic: ResearchTopic,
  prompt: string,
  systemPrompt: string,
): Promise<Finding> {
  const MAX_APP_RETRIES = 1; // one extra app-level retry beyond SDK's maxRetries
  let attempt = 0;

  while (attempt <= MAX_APP_RETRIES) {
    try {
      const result = await generateText({
        model: anthropic('claude-sonnet-4-6'),
        system: systemPrompt,
        prompt,
        output: Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema }),
        maxOutputTokens: 512,
        temperature: 0.1,
        maxRetries: 2, // SDK retries on transient provider errors
      });

      if (!result.output) {
        // Schema validation passed but output is unexpectedly null — treat as sentinel
        console.warn(`[research] Empty output for topic ${topic.id} on attempt ${attempt}`);
        return NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topic.id, 'Empty structured output from model');
      }

      return result.output;
    } catch (err) {
      if (err instanceof AI_NoObjectGeneratedError) {
        // Log the raw text for debugging hallucination patterns
        console.error(`[research] Schema validation failed (attempt ${attempt}):`, {
          topicId: topic.id,
          rawText: err.text?.slice(0, 500), // first 500 chars only
          cause:   String(err.cause),
          usage:   err.usage,
        });

        if (attempt < MAX_APP_RETRIES) {
          attempt++;
          continue; // retry with same prompt
        }

        // After all retries: write explicit absence rather than crashing the job
        return NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topic.id, 'Schema validation failed after retries');
      }
      throw err; // non-schema errors (network, auth) propagate to job-level handler
    }
  }

  // Unreachable but satisfies TypeScript
  return NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL(topic.id, 'Unexpected retry exhaustion');
}
```

**Retry policy:**
- SDK `maxRetries: 2` handles transient provider/network errors automatically
- App-level retry (`MAX_APP_RETRIES = 1`) handles schema validation failures with the same prompt
- After all retries: write the sentinel, do not crash — partial findings are preserved (D-05)
- Log `err.text` (the raw model output) on every schema failure — this is the diagnostic signal for prompt tuning

### Async-First Design

AI SDK v5's `generateText` returns a `Promise` and must be `await`ed. In the Next.js App Router context:

- **Route Handlers are async by default** when exported with `async function POST(req: Request)`. Never forget `await` inside the handler body — the route returns before the AI call completes if you do.
- **The research worker is fire-and-forget from the server action** (D-03). The action calls `fetch('POST /api/research/run')` without awaiting the response. The route handler `await`s the full worker inside its own async scope. This is intentional and correct — do not add `await` at the action level.
- **Do not use streaming** (`streamText`, `streamObject`) for this system. The background worker writes to a database, not a UI stream. Streaming would complicate the per-topic commit logic without benefit. Use `await generateText(...)` and commit each finding synchronously within the topic loop.

```typescript
// CORRECT: fire-and-forget at the server action level
// src/lib/actions/research-actions.ts
'use server';
export async function triggerResearch(firmId: number) {
  const job = await enqueueJob({ firmId });
  // No await here — intentionally fire-and-forget (D-03)
  fetch(`${process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL}/api/research/run`, {
    method: 'POST',
    body: JSON.stringify({ firmId, jobId: job.id }),
    headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' },
  }).catch(err => console.error('[research] Failed to trigger worker:', err));
  return job.id;
}

// CORRECT: the route handler awaits the full worker
// src/app/api/research/run/route.ts
export async function POST(req: Request) {
  const { firmId, jobId } = await req.json();
  await updateJobStatus(jobId, 'running');
  try {
    await runResearchWorker(firmId, jobId, new TavilySearchClient());
    await updateJobStatus(jobId, 'done');
    await db.update(firms).set({ lastResearchedAt: new Date() }).where(eq(firms.id, firmId));
  } catch (err) {
    const msg = err instanceof Error ? err.message : String(err);
    await updateJobStatus(jobId, 'failed', msg);
  }
  return Response.json({ ok: true });
}
```

**The one common mistake:** wrapping `generateText` in a `Promise.all` across all 7 topics to parallelise. This multiplies concurrent Anthropic API calls (7× per firm, N firms if triggered simultaneously), causes rate limit errors (Anthropic's default tier), and makes partial-failure handling complex. The sequential topic loop is intentional — it is fast enough (7 × ~3s ≈ 21s per firm) and stays within free-tier Tavily limits.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt separation** — The system prompt sets the persona, rules, and output contract. The user prompt carries the per-topic grounding context and instruction. Never mix them.

```typescript
// src/lib/research/prompts.ts

export const RESEARCH_SYSTEM_PROMPT = `
You are a UK firm intelligence analyst supporting bid/consortium planning for civil nuclear and defence programmes.

Your task is to synthesise findings about a specific firm on a specific topic, drawing on:
1. Verified UK structured data (Companies House profile, PSC register, UK procurement records) provided in the grounding context.
2. Web search results provided below.

Output rules (enforced by schema — do not deviate):
- confidence HIGH: use only when the finding is drawn directly from the provided structured UK API data. Set sourceUrl to the data source identifier.
- confidence MEDIUM: use when the finding is AI-derived from a cited Tavily search result. sourceUrl MUST be a real URL from the provided search results — not invented.
- Never fabricate sourceUrl, sourceTitle, or rawSnippet. If you cannot attribute a finding to a provided source, emit the not-found sentinel pattern described in the user prompt.
- rawSnippet must be a verbatim excerpt from the source, not a paraphrase.
`.trim();

export function buildTopicPrompt(
  topic: ResearchTopic,
  ctx: GroundingContext,
  searchResults: WebSearchResult[],
): string {
  const groundingBlock = formatGroundingContext(ctx);   // CH profile + top 10 OCDS records
  const searchBlock    = formatSearchResults(searchResults);

  return `
## Firm: ${ctx.firmName}
## Topic: ${topic.label}

### Grounding Context (UK Structured Data — verified, confidence HIGH)
${groundingBlock}

### Web Search Results (Tavily — cite by URL, confidence MEDIUM)
${searchBlock}

### Instruction
Synthesise a finding for topic "${topic.label}" about ${ctx.firmName}.
If no reliable source supports a finding on this topic, respond with:
  summary: "Not found: <brief reason>"
  confidence: HIGH
  sourceUrl: "not-found"
  sourceTitle: "Research worker"
  rawSnippet: "No verifiable source found."
`.trim();
}
```

**Few-shot guidance** — For this system type (structured extraction from provided context), few-shot examples are not needed in the system prompt: the Zod schema enforced by `Output.object()` is a stronger contract than prompt examples. If you observe systematic misattribution (e.g. the model invents `sourceUrl` values), add one negative few-shot example showing the not-found sentinel pattern, not positive examples.

**Token budget** — Always set `maxOutputTokens: 512` explicitly. A 3-field citation finding never requires more. Leaving it unbounded in production allows a runaway response to cost 10–50× the expected amount.

### Context Window Management

This system type (bounded structured extraction) has straightforward context management because the grounding context is assembled, not retrieved dynamically. Apply these rules in `buildGroundingContext`:

**Grounding context budget (per call):**

| Block | Token budget | Rule |
|-------|-------------|------|
| System prompt | ~300 | Fixed; do not grow it |
| CH profile + PSC summary | ~200 | Always include; format as compact key-value pairs |
| OCDS procurement records | ~500 | Top 10 by recency; truncate record titles > 80 chars |
| Tavily search results | ~750 | Up to 5 results × 150 tokens per snippet |
| Per-topic instruction | ~100 | Fixed |
| **Total input** | **~1,850** | Well within claude-sonnet-4-6's 200k context window |

**Truncation strategy** — If OCDS records exceed budget (a firm with 100+ contracts), select top 10 by `awardDate DESC`, not by arbitrary truncation. Include a note in the grounding block: "Showing 10 of N procurement records (most recent)." The model can reference this in its summary.

**What not to do** — Do not pass the full raw OCDS JSON payload to the model. Format it into a human-readable table or bullet list first. Raw JSON bloats the context and degrades synthesis quality. Do this formatting in `grounding.ts`, not in the prompt.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> This system's "good" is dominated by **data-integrity invariants** that are deterministically checkable in code (citation completeness, provenance separation, explicit absence). Code-based checks are the first and primary line of evaluation. LLM-judge and human review are reserved for the genuinely subjective dimensions where code cannot decide — citation *precision* (does the URL actually contain the snippet?), entity disambiguation, and minority-PSC-nationality surfacing. This ordering matches the domain: a bid manager rejects a finding for a structural defect (no source, wrong provenance tag) long before they argue about narrative quality.

### Dimensions

> Each dimension maps to a rubric ingredient in Section 1b and a critical failure mode in Section 1. Rubric language is the bid manager's accept/reject judgement, restated as a Pass/Fail criterion the eval harness asserts.

| # | Dimension | Rubric (PASS / FAIL) | Measurement | Priority |
|---|-----------|----------------------|-------------|----------|
| E1 | **Citation completeness** (FM1, D-11, RES-03) | PASS: every row written to `findings` has non-empty `sourceUrl`, `sourceTitle`, `rawSnippet` (each `.min(1)`); no LOW confidence rows exist. FAIL: any stored finding has an empty/missing citation field, or a LOW-confidence row was persisted. | **Code** — Zod schema assertion + post-run SQL query over `findings`. | Critical |
| E2 | **Explicit absence** (FM2, D-09/D-11, RES-05) | PASS: for every one of the 7 topics, the firm has at least one row after a completed run — a real finding OR a `sourceUrl = "not-found"` sentinel with a non-empty reason in `summary`. FAIL: any topic has zero rows for the firm after the job reaches `done`. | **Code** — assert `COUNT(DISTINCT topicId) == 7` per firm per completed job. | Critical |
| E3 | **Provenance separation** (FM3, D-09, RES-04) | PASS: HIGH is used only for findings traceable to structured UK API data (CH / CF / FTS) or the not-found sentinel; MEDIUM is used only for AI-derived findings whose `sourceUrl` is a real Tavily result URL. FAIL: a Tavily-sourced narrative claim is tagged HIGH, or a structured-API fact is tagged MEDIUM, or HIGH and AI-narrative are mixed in one `summary`. | **Code** (provenance/URL-origin checks) + **LLM Judge** (detects HIGH/MEDIUM/narrative mixing inside `summary`). | Critical |
| E4 | **Citation non-fabrication** (FM4) | PASS: every MEDIUM finding's `sourceUrl` exactly matches a URL present in the Tavily results passed to that call. FAIL: `sourceUrl` is a URL the model invented or altered (not in the retrieved result set). | **Code** — set-membership check of `sourceUrl` against captured Tavily result URLs (requires the run to log retrieved URLs). | Critical |
| E5 | **Citation precision** (Section 1b, RES-03) | PASS: opening `sourceUrl` and searching for `rawSnippet` confirms the snippet appears (verbatim or near-verbatim) on that page, and the page is the specific page for the claim — not a homepage. FAIL: `rawSnippet` is a paraphrase not on the page, or `sourceUrl` is a generic homepage rather than the claim's page. | **Human** (v1 primary, ground truth) + **LLM Judge** (scaled, calibrated against human) — "does rawSnippet substantively appear at sourceUrl, and is the URL claim-specific?" | High |
| E6 | **Entity disambiguation** (Section 1b, FM-domain 2/5, DATA-04) | PASS: the finding is unambiguously about the researched legal entity — references the Companies House number where the claim is registry/procurement-derived, and does not attribute a sibling/parent/dissolved-predecessor entity's contract to the firm. FAIL: a contract or claim about a similarly-named or related entity is presented as the firm's. | **LLM Judge** (flags entity mismatch against the firm name + CH number in grounding) + **Human** (edge cases). | High |
| E7 | **Source currency / staleness honesty** (Section 1b, FM-domain 1) | PASS: ownership/PSC and filing-derived findings state the filing/snapshot date and do not assert current truth beyond what the dated source supports. FAIL: a finding asserts "the firm is UK-owned" (or similar) with no cited filing date, treating a point-in-time snapshot as current fact. | **LLM Judge** (checks for an explicit date qualifier on currency-sensitive findings) + **Human** (calibration). | High |
| E8 | **Minority non-UK PSC surfacing** (Section 1b, FM-domain 3; NSI/SC-DV context) | PASS: where grounding shows any non-UK PSC, the ownership finding names the non-UK nationality individually (not just a "primarily UK-controlled" majority summary). FAIL: a minority non-UK PSC present in grounding is absent from the surfaced finding. | **LLM Judge** (compares finding against PSC nationalities in grounding) + **Human** (contracts-lawyer review for NSI accuracy). | High |
| E9 | **Job lifecycle integrity** (FM5, D-03/D-05, RES-02) | PASS: every started job reaches `done` or `failed` (never stuck `running`); on partial failure, already-written findings persist and the job is `failed` with a non-empty error message. FAIL: a job remains `running` with no terminal transition, or partial findings are lost on failure. | **Code** — integration test asserting terminal state and partial-findings retention (inject a mid-run error). | Critical |

### Eval Tooling

**Primary Tool:** **Promptfoo** (open-source CLI, TypeScript-native config) for prompt-level regression + LLM-judge assertions, paired with **Vitest** code-based validators for the deterministic invariants (E1–E4, E9). This is a local-first single-user TypeScript tool — a self-hostable CLI with no platform account is the correct fit. (Arize Phoenix is the GSD default but is Python/OpenTelemetry-centric; it is not adopted here because the stack is pure TypeScript and the dominant eval surface is code-based assertions, not trace visualisation. Rationale for not using RAGAS: this is not a semantic-retrieval RAG system (CLAUDE.md: "No vector DB / RAG infra for v1"). RAGAS's retrieval-precision/recall metrics do not apply; the retrieval surface here is fixed (1–2 Tavily calls per topic) and citation correctness is a set-membership check, not a relevance score.

**Setup:**
```bash
# Promptfoo for prompt regression + LLM-judge assertions (CLI, no account)

# LLM-judge assertions reuse the existing Anthropic key
```

**CI/CD Integration:**

The Vitest invariant suite is a **blocking** gate (any failure of E1/E2/E3-code/E4/E9 fails the build — these are the catastrophic data-integrity modes). The Promptfoo judge suite is a **threshold** gate (e.g. require ≥ 90% pass across the dataset for E5–E8) so a single subjective miss does not block, but a regression trend does.

## 6. Guardrails

> This is a local-first internal tool, not a public-facing product — there is **no content-safety / toxicity / abuse surface** to guard. Stakes are nonetheless Critical (multi-million-to-billion GBP bid decisions). Guardrails therefore target **data integrity at the write boundary**: nothing unverifiable, mis-attributed, or silently absent should ever reach the `findings` table the bid manager reads. "Online" here means *runs on every finding before it is persisted*, inside the worker's per-topic loop — the equivalent of a per-request guardrail for a background worker.

### Online (Real-Time — enforced per finding, before DB write)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Schema citation gate** (FM1, E1) | Any finding missing/empty `sourceUrl`, `sourceTitle`, or `rawSnippet`, or with `confidence` outside {HIGH, MEDIUM}. | **Block write** — `Output.object({ schema: FindingSchema })` (`.min(1)` fields) rejects it; on `AI_NoObjectGeneratedError`/empty output, substitute the `NOT_FOUND_SENTINEL` (never persist the invalid finding). |
| **No-LOW gate** (FM1, D-09) | Model emits or implies a LOW-confidence (AI-derived, unverifiable) finding. | **Block + replace** — coerce to the not-found sentinel; LOW is never a storable state (enforced in code, not just prompt). |
| **Citation-origin gate** (FM4, E4) | A MEDIUM finding's `sourceUrl` is not a member of the Tavily result URLs captured for that call. | **Block + replace** with not-found sentinel (the URL was fabricated/altered); log `firmId/topicId` + the offending URL for the flywheel. |
| **Absence-coverage gate** (FM2, E2) | A topic loop completes without writing any row for that topic. | **Force sentinel** — the loop guarantees exactly one write per topic (real finding or sentinel); a topic can never end with zero rows. |
| **Job-terminal gate** (FM5, E9) | Worker throws or exits without a terminal job transition. | **Escalate to `failed`** — outer `POST /api/research/run` handler always calls `updateJobStatus(jobId, 'failed', msg)` in `catch`; partial findings retained (D-05). No job stays `running`. |

### Offline (Flywheel — sampled batch, feeds prompt/worker refinement)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Citation precision rate** (E5) | Human-verify a random sample of MEDIUM findings per run (e.g. 5 per full 12-firm run); weight toward findings whose `sourceUrl` host == firm homepage (precision-risk signal). | If precision drops below ~90%, tighten the system-prompt rule that `rawSnippet` must be verbatim and `sourceUrl` must be the claim's specific page; add a negative few-shot. |
| **Provenance-mixing rate** (E3) | LLM-judge over a sample of HIGH+narrative findings; weight toward findings where `confidence = HIGH` but `sourceUrl != "not-found"` and is a web URL (mis-tag signal). | If mixing recurs, split the prompt to emit structured-fact and AI-narrative findings as separate rows rather than one summary. |
| **Sentinel rate per topic** (E2, quality signal) | Track % of findings that are not-found sentinels, by topic, across runs. | A topic that is *always* sentinel signals a bad search query template (D-10) or grounding gap — revise the query builder, not the model. A sudden spike signals Tavily/provider degradation — investigate the pipeline. |
| **Fabricated-URL incidence** (E4) | Count citation-origin gate rejections per run (the gate blocks them online; the *rate* is the flywheel signal). | A rising rate means the model is increasingly inventing URLs — add the not-found-sentinel negative few-shot and reaffirm "sourceUrl MUST be from provided results" in the system prompt. |
| **Entity-mismatch incidence** (E6) | LLM-judge sample weighted toward firms with shared group branding (F5/F6/F7 class) and firms where a Tavily result host does not match the firm's known domains. | If mismatches recur, add the CH number prominently to the search query and grounding header, and instruct the model to reject results that name a different legal entity. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

> "Production" for v1 is a single user running a local Next.js app — there is no multi-tenant traffic, no paging, and no SLA. Monitoring is therefore lightweight and local: structured logs + a queryable run-history view over the data the worker already writes (`jobs`, `findings`). The goal is operability ("did the run succeed, and can I trust what it wrote?"), not high-availability ops.

**Tracing Tool:** Lightweight **OpenTelemetry-style structured logging** to a local file/console, plus a SQL-queryable history over the existing `jobs` and `findings` tables — no external tracing platform for v1. Each `generateText` call logs one structured record: `{ jobId, firmId, topicId, model, inputTokens, outputTokens, latencyMs, confidence, sourceUrlHost, wasSentinel, schemaRetryCount }`. Arize Phoenix / Langfuse self-hosted is deferred to a future cloud deployment (the fire-and-forget worker is already Railway-extractable per D-03; tracing instrumentation slots in at that point via OpenTelemetry without changing the worker logic).

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Job outcome rate** — % of jobs reaching `done` vs `failed` vs (alarm:) stuck `running`. Derived from `jobs.status`. (FM5)
2. **Sentinel ratio** — not-found sentinels as a fraction of all findings, overall and per topic. A rising ratio means research quality is degrading (thin search results, grounding gaps, or provider issues). (E2)
3. **Online-gate rejection counts per run** — fabricated-URL rejections (E4), no-LOW coercions, schema-validation failures (`schemaRetryCount > 0`). These are the early-warning signals that the model's citation behaviour is drifting. (FM1, FM4)
4. **Cost & latency per run** — summed input/output tokens and total wall-clock per firm and per full run, against the Section 4 budget (~$0.06/firm, ~21s/firm, ~$0.76/run). (Cost/latency drift.)
5. **Provider error rate** — non-schema exceptions (network/auth/rate-limit) surfaced through SDK `maxRetries` exhaustion, which drive jobs to `failed`.

**Alert Thresholds:** (surfaced in-app / in logs — no external paging for a local single-user tool)
- **Any** job in `running` with no terminal transition after a worker run completes → flag as a freeze (FM5 — this should be impossible by design; if it occurs, the job-terminal gate has a bug).
- Sentinel ratio for a single full run > ~40% → flag "low yield" (likely search/grounding/provider problem, not model failure).
- Fabricated-URL rejections > 0 in a run → flag for prompt review (the online gate already blocked the write; the alert drives the flywheel fix).
- Per-run cost or latency > 2× the Section 4 budget → flag cost/latency drift (likely `maxOutputTokens` regression or runaway retries).
- Any provider error rate spike (multiple jobs `failed` with provider errors) → flag pipeline/provider issue, advise re-trigger.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:** Weight human review (E5/E6/E7/E8) toward the interactions most likely to be wrong — do not sample uniformly:
- Findings where `confidence = HIGH` but `sourceUrl` is a web URL (provenance mis-tag risk — E3).
- MEDIUM findings whose `sourceUrl` host equals the firm's homepage (citation-precision risk — E5).
- Findings for firms with shared group branding / multiple CH-registered entities (entity-confusion risk — E6).
- Ownership/PSC findings for firms whose grounding shows any non-UK PSC (NSI / SC-DV stakes — E8; route these to the contracts-lawyer role from Section 1b).
- Runs that produced an above-baseline sentinel ratio or any online-gate rejection (something concerning already fired — inspect the whole run).
